## World premise, tone, and era

In the present day, an adult archivist returns to the long-vacant house on Stillwater Road and opens a damp cardboard box hidden beneath the stairs. Its contents suggest that a child named June once lived in the house, although family photographs, school records, and the archivist’s own conscious memory insist otherwise. The house repeatedly preserves June’s place: a second chair appears in images, a water glass is set for someone no one names, and handmade stars emerge from sealed spaces. The three-episode excerpt establishes that June was real and that something happened beside Route 17 at 4:17 p.m., but it withholds whether she died, disappeared, or was deliberately removed.

- **Story-present era:** 2026.
- **Artifact era:** primarily 1988–1989.
- **Visual tone:** faded consumer film, dust, late-afternoon light, overexposed windows, damp paper, ordinary rooms held too still.
- **Narrative tone:** intimate, credible, understated; captions never claim the supernatural as settled fact.
- **Mystery ratio for this excerpt:** roughly 70% atmosphere, 20% story, 10% solvable clue.

## Location

### The Stillhouse, 17 Stillwater Road

A narrow two-story family house at the edge of fictional Stillwater County. The fixture treats it as one location with four addressable subareas:

- **Under-stair cupboard:** archive-box discovery point; dry wall surrounds one inexplicably damp floorboard.
- **Kitchen:** blue chair, water glass, family table, and the visual proof that one place was repeatedly removed.
- **Upstairs small room:** white curtains, nail holes shaped like a constellation, no furniture in the present.
- **Back threshold:** view toward Route 17; two child-height marks remain on the jamb, one painted over.

The location must remain spatially stable across all three episodes.

## Season hidden truth — creator-only

On **April 17, 1989, at 4:17 p.m.**, June nearly drowned in the pool of the fictional **Red Current Motor Lodge** beside Route 17 after being left without adult supervision. She survived and recovered in Room 217 of the Stillwater County Recovery Annex. To conceal the neglect and avoid a public inquiry involving a locally influential relative, the family sent June to live with a distant aunt under the aunt’s surname. Mara was repeatedly told that June had been imaginary until that explanation displaced much of her own memory. Their mother later hid a small evidence box under the stairs so the truth would outlive the family’s silence.

The mini-season excerpt must not reveal the transfer, the new surname, the responsible adult, or June’s present status.

## Recurring symbols and secret meaning

### Blue chair

- **Surface meaning:** ordinary child’s furniture.
- **Secret meaning:** the place a family can remove from a photograph but not from a room’s repeated arrangement.
- **Cadence:** visible or verbally referenced in every episode.

### Glass of water

- **Surface meaning:** mundane domestic object.
- **Secret meaning:** truth held in plain sight; water is both the site of harm and the family’s ritual of pretending everything is normal.
- **Cadence:** still water in episode 1, audible glass in episode 2, spilled water in episode 3.

### Paper star

- **Surface meaning:** child’s craft.
- **Secret meaning:** June’s self-authored proof of existence; a small record made by someone adults attempted to edit out.
- **Cadence:** fragment in episode 1, opened in episode 2, constellation-like nail pattern in episode 3.

### Secondary motifs

- **4:17 / 417:** the moment memory split into before and after.
- **Red float:** failed protection and adult negligence.
- **White curtains:** institutional quiet presented as innocence.

## Mini chronology — story time

1. **November 1988:** June and Mara make paper stars at the kitchen table; a photograph captures both blue chairs.
2. **April 17, 1989, 4:17 p.m.:** June is pulled from the Red Current Motor Lodge pool beside Route 17 and transported conscious.
3. **April 20, 1989:** June leaves Room 217 in a private transfer; family photographs and household references begin to be altered.
4. **July 2026:** The Archivist returns to the Stillhouse, opens the under-stair box, and begins publishing the archive.

## Three-episode excerpt

### Episode 1 — “The Second Chair”

- Archive box is opened.
- Kitchen photograph and present-day kitchen are compared.
- Audience notices the second blue chair, water glass, and paper-star corner.
- Final line: **“There was room for someone the photograph no longer names.”**
- Exit audience state: another child may have lived there, but no name or event is known.

### Episode 2 — “June Was Here”

- Folded paper star is opened.
- Cassette fragment confirms two child voices.
- The Archivist admits that “June” was the name adults called imaginary.
- Final line: **“An imaginary friend does not leave handwriting in a sealed wall.”**
- Exit audience state: June existed, knew Mara, and occupied the blue chair.

### Episode 3 — “The Water Line”

- Dispatch carbon is reconstructed.
- Key tag 217 is matched to a cropped white-curtain photograph.
- The Archivist states that June was her sister.
- Final panel shows the red float’s split seam but withholds the outcome.
- Final line: **“The record ends at transport. The house does not.”**
- Exit audience state: June was Mara’s sister and experienced a water emergency on Route 17 at 4:17; Room 217 is connected; survival and deliberate erasure remain hidden.

## Audience knowledge state
