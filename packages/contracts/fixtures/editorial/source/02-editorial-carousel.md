## Voice rules

1. **Specific but not exposing:** begin with an ordinary image, then generalize the lesson; never preserve details that could identify the source situation.
2. **Firm without accusation:** name choices and consequences, not diagnoses, labels, or character verdicts.
3. **Humble authority:** use “I learned,” “I noticed,” or “sometimes,” rather than universal commands or claims of expertise.
4. **Hope without guaranteed reconciliation:** repair is possible, but no panel promises access, forgiveness, or reunion.
5. **Dignity test:** a person represented by the private source should be able to read the public piece without feeling publicly cornered or exposed.

## Lesson / thesis

**A boundary is not a punishment. It is the repair line that makes safe closeness possible.**

The piece distinguishes access from connection: unrestricted access can continue harm, while a clear limit can create the conditions in which trust might later return.

## Recurring metaphor and evolution

### The garden gate

1. **Panel 1:** a gate is stuck and someone keeps pushing harder.
2. **Panels 2–3:** the bent hinge is revealed; force is worsening the damage.
3. **Panels 4–6:** the gate is taken off its hinge, cleaned, aligned, and repaired.
4. **Panels 7–8:** the gate can open, but only when someone on each side chooses to meet without forcing it.
5. **Panel 9:** the gate rests closed but functional; closed is not broken, and open is not owed.

Secret editorial meaning: the boundary is the hinge repair, not the wall.

## Nine-panel outline

1. **Hook:** “Sometimes we keep pushing on a gate that is telling us the hinge is hurt.”
2. **Reframe:** “More force does not create more closeness.”
3. **Name the limit:** “A boundary says: we cannot keep using the damaged hinge this way.”
4. **Remove punishment framing:** “It is not a lock installed to make someone suffer.”
5. **Define repair:** “Repair looks like calm words, truthful ownership, and a pattern that changes after the apology.”
6. **Protect dignity:** “You can care about someone and still stop the motion that keeps injuring both of you.”
7. **Mutuality:** “A healthy gate opens from either side; it is not kicked in from one.”
8. **Uncertain hope:** “Time may make welcome possible. It may also confirm that distance is wiser.”
9. **Closing thesis:** “The goal is not access. The goal is safety enough for welcome.”
