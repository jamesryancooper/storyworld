## Property premise

A father’s visual notebook about love, boundaries, adulthood, responsibility, healing, family repair, and hope. It may be emotionally specific, but it must never function as a public accusation, covert message about a live dispute, demand for reconciliation, or exposure of a real daughter’s private history.

## Voice rules

1. **Firm truth, softened by love.** Direct language is allowed; humiliation is not.
2. The father owns his feelings and choices. He does not narrate the daughter’s motives as fact.
3. Trauma, pain, or fear may explain a wall, but no panel diagnoses the recipient or reduces her to a wound.
4. Love does not become rescue, control, surveillance, financial leverage, or entitlement to access.
5. Hope remains open without promising that the relationship will return to a preferred form.
6. The public lesson must be useful beyond this fictional family and contain no recognizable private incident.

## Visual grammar

- 4:5 portrait, full-bleed warm cream textured paper.
- Handmade emotional-sketchbook / bullet-journal language.
- Black ink, graphite, muted watercolor, restrained red underline, taped paper, arrows, circles, small margin notes.
- No visible notebook spine, crease, binding, desk, tabletop, page edge, or environment outside the page.
- Recurring symbols: wall bricks, loose brick, gate, bridge planks, porch light, red thread, folded note, repaired crack, small seedling.
- Series title **must not appear as headline or footer on the public panels**. Series association remains metadata or account context.
- Text is a controlled overlay layer; illustration generation must not be trusted to reproduce it exactly.

## Lesson / thesis

**Closeness has to feel safe. Love can keep the porch light on while trust is rebuilt slowly, but one person cannot force another person’s gate open.**

## Metaphor evolution

`wall` → `loose brick` → `gate` → `bridge` → `porch light`

- The wall acknowledges protection and distance.
- The loose brick represents one honest admission.
- The gate represents chosen, bounded contact.
- The bridge represents trust built from repeated actions.
- The porch light represents enduring love without forced entry.

## Ten-panel outline and exact overlay copy

### Panel 1 — Wall

**“There’s a wall between us.”**

Margin note: **This is not blame. This is me naming what hurts.**

### Panel 2 — Time

**“It wasn’t built in one day.”**

Small note: **Neither will repair be.**

### Panel 3 — Distortion

**“Love sounds different through a wall.”**

Art: two speech bubbles reaching the wall and becoming faint, not hostile.

### Panel 4 — Protection and cost

**“The wall may have protected you. Now it also keeps repair out.”**

The art must not suggest that protection was foolish or that the daughter owes access.

### Panel 5 — Father’s limit

**“I cannot tear it down for you without becoming another force against you.”**

Art: father steps back from a sledgehammer-shaped shadow; no weapon in his hand.

### Panel 6 — Honest opening

**“One honest brick can move.”**

Margin examples: **“I was hurt.” “I was afraid.” “I handled that badly.”**

### Panel 7 — Gate

**“A gate is not surrender. It is a safer way to choose contact.”**

Art: a small gate fitted into the wall; latch accessible from both sides.

### Panel 8 — Trust materials

**“Trust is built with calm words, kept promises, and changed patterns.”**

Three bridge planks carry the labels `calm words`, `kept promises`, and `changed patterns`.

### Panel 9 — Bridge

**“Small openings can become a bridge.”**

Margin note: **Only when both people keep building.**

### Panel 10 — Porch light

**“The porch light is still on. The door has to open from your side too.”**

Closing annotation: **Love without rescue. Hope without force.**

## Caption style

- 120–220 words.
- Written as a universal fatherly reflection, not as a letter containing a private demand.
- No name, date, city, screenshot, quotation from private communication, or reference to a specific crisis.
- Ends with a grounded line rather than a question asking readers to disclose family trauma.
- Suggested closing: **“A boundary is not the end of love. Sometimes it is the line where honest repair finally has room to begin.”**
