## Property premise

**Dumpster Fire Friends** is an original illustrated cautionary-card anthology about recognizable social and emotional behavior patterns. Each character is funny, messy, and human. The joke targets the pattern and its consequences, not a protected trait, diagnosis, trauma history, or immutable identity. Every set moves from recognition to accountability, a healthier response, and a recovery path.

## Property tone rules

- Funny, sharp, wise, slightly dark, and emotionally intelligent.
- Satire may exaggerate props and situations but must not portray a character as subhuman or irredeemable.
- No diagnosis, pseudo-diagnosis, therapy jargon used as a weapon, or claim that a behavior proves a disorder.
- No protected-trait mockery, body ridicule, cruelty, shame campaign, or invitation to harass someone.
- “What may be underneath” can supply context but never erase accountability.
- Advice must emphasize boundaries, accurate language, repair, and choice rather than retaliation.
- The property must remain visually and linguistically original; do not imitate an existing collectible-card franchise.

## Shared card-layout invariants

- Format: 4:5 portrait.
- Every slide in a character set repeats the same card number in the same anchored location.
- Every set may use flexible topic-specific headings, but the semantic sequence remains stable:
  1. Front character
  2. Stats
  3. Known behaviors
  4. Scenario / spiral
  5. Damage report
  6. Understanding / what is underneath
  7. Self-reflection
  8. Better behavior or language
  9. Healthy response guide
  10. Healing / recovery
  11. Lesson
- Final text is a manual overlay layer; generated art must not be trusted to render exact copy.
- Shared style: distressed cream card stock, smoke gray, burnt orange, deep purple, toxic green accents, hand-inked comic illustration, warning labels, small dumpster-fire emblem.
- Characters share collection membership but are not assumed to know one another. `collection_member_of` is not a personal relationship edge.

# Character Set A — Reality-Revision Riley

## Stable identity

- **Card number:** 007.
- **Name:** Reality-Revision Riley.
- **Pronouns:** they/them in fixture copy; presentation remains gender-neutral.
- **Subtitle:** **The Human Edit Button**.
- **Taxonomy:** conflict evasion / reality distortion / accountability avoidance.
- **Core pattern:** when an uncomfortable fact threatens Riley’s preferred story, Riley denies, minimizes, reframes, changes the subject, or pressures the other person to doubt their own memory.
- **What Riley knows:** some facts were omitted or changed.
- **What Riley wrongly believes:** admitting one false statement means surrendering all dignity and control.
- **What may be underneath:** shame and fear of losing status; this explains pressure but does not excuse distortion.

## Recurring visual motifs

- Red editing pencil.
- Giant eraser.
- Crossed-out speech bubbles.
- Portable fog machine.
- Warped clocks and rearranged timeline cards.
- Cracked mirror.
- Exit door.
- Lantern, compass, and “truth signal” used only in recovery slides.

## Eleven-card outline and overlay copy

### 1. Front Character — “Reality-Revision Riley”

- Headline: **REALITY-REVISION RILEY**
- Subtitle: **THE HUMAN EDIT BUTTON**
- Warning strip: **MAY REARRANGE FACTS WHEN ACCOUNTABILITY ENTERS THE ROOM**
- Art: Riley holds a red pencil over a speech bubble while a small fog machine runs beside a cracked mirror.

### 2. Reality Meter

- Show the five fixed stats.
- Margin note: **“Confidence: loud. Accuracy: under revision.”**

### 3. Pattern Sightings

Approved recurring examples:

- “That never happened.”
- “You are remembering it wrong.”
- “Why are you making this such a big deal?”
- “I was joking. You always take things badly.”

Footer: **A changed story is still a change.**

### 4. The Fog Machine — conversation spiral

One-line sequence:

**Question → denial → minimization → counter-accusation → demand that the other person apologize.**

Overlay line: **“If Riley cannot win the story, Riley rewrites the room.”**

### 5. Reality Damage Report

- People begin saving receipts for ordinary conversations.
- They over-explain simple memories.
- They stop trusting their own judgment.
- Conflict lasts longer because the shared facts keep moving.

Warning label: **CONFUSION IS AN IMPACT, NOT PROOF THAT THE FACTS WERE UNCLEAR.**

### 6. Under the Smoke

- Riley may fear shame, rejection, or loss of control.
- Context line: **“Fear can explain the fog. It does not make the fog honest.”**
- Art should show Riley smaller and less theatrical, with the fog-machine switch visible in their own hand.

### 7. Reality Check

Self-reflection prompts:

- “What fact am I trying to make disappear?”
- “What part do I know I changed?”
- “Am I asking for understanding, or demanding that someone abandon their memory?”

Overlay truth: **THE TRUTH DOES NOT NEED A FOG MACHINE.**

### 8. New Move: Tell the Truth

Approved replacement language:

> “I said it. I became defensive. Here is what I remember, and I may have missed something. I am willing to compare the facts without attacking you.”

Visual: red pencil set down; one speech bubble restored rather than crossed out.

### 9. Hold the Line — healthy response guide

Approved response:

> “I am not going to debate whether I experienced what I experienced. We can talk when the facts stay put and neither of us is pressured to surrender our memory.”

Safety note: disengaging from a circular conversation is not rude.

### 10. Rebuild Your Signal

- Write down important events while memory is fresh.
- Check interpretation with a trusted, uninvolved person.
- Separate facts, feelings, and assumptions.
- Leave conversations that repeatedly move the facts.
- Return only when calm, safe, and useful.

Art: lantern and compass cut through thinning smoke; the cracked mirror remains repaired but visibly lined.

### 11. The Lesson Under the Smoke

Primary line: **“The truth does not need a fog machine.”**

Secondary line: **“Repair begins when the story stops moving long enough for responsibility to enter.”**

## Character-state progression

`performing certainty` → `challenged` → `escalating distortion` → `impact visible` → `recognizes choice` → `tries accurate language` → `repair possible, not guaranteed`

The final card does not automatically mark Riley “healed.” It marks one healthier behavior as available.

# Character Set B — Doomscroll Daisy

## Stable identity

- **Card number:** 008.
- **Name:** Doomscroll Daisy.
- **Subtitle:** **The Panic-Refresh Prophet**.
- **Taxonomy:** attention capture / catastrophic consumption / passive alarm loop.
- **Core pattern:** consumes negative updates until anxiety becomes a daily environment, then treats every new headline as an immediate personal emergency.
- **What Daisy knows:** refreshing rarely changes the event.
- **What Daisy wrongly believes:** constant monitoring is the same as being responsible and prepared.

## Recurring visual motifs

- Oversized phone.
- Red notification wheel.
- Storm cloud made of headlines with invented text.
- Hourglass leaking tiny alert icons.
- Window showing the ordinary world continuing outside.
- Small “OFF” switch introduced in recovery cards.

## Stats — fixed values for Card #008

| Stat | Value |
|---|---:|
| Refresh rate | 10 / 10 |
| Context retained | 3 / 10 |
| Sleep protected | 1 / 10 |
| Useful action taken | 2 / 10 |
| Ability to log off at start | 2 / 10 |

## Eleven-card one-line outline

1. **Front:** Daisy sits in a notification storm; warning: **MAY TURN EVERY UPDATE INTO A PERSONAL SIREN.**
2. **Stats:** display fixed values; note: **“Informed: maybe. Rested: absolutely not.”**
3. **Known Behaviors:** refreshes before getting out of bed, reads comments as evidence, forwards alarms without checking context.
4. **The Panic-Refresh Spiral:** headline → refresh → worse headline → imagined catastrophe → more refreshing.
5. **Attention Damage Report:** sleep shrinks, ordinary tasks feel irresponsible, relationships receive only leftover attention.
6. **Under the Alarm:** uncertainty feels intolerable; constant checking creates the illusion of control.
7. **Signal Check:** “Did this update give me a useful action, or only a stronger feeling?”
8. **New Move:** choose two check-in windows, verify source, name one action, close the feed.
9. **Healthy Response Guide:** friends can refuse panic-forwarding while offering a calmer summary or practical step.
10. **Reclaim the Room:** restore sleep, meals, movement, local relationships, and time without incoming alerts.
11. **Dumpster Fire Lesson:** **“Being informed has an ending. Being consumed does not.”**
