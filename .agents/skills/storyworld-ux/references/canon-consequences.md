# Canon, consequences, and creative provenance

Use this protocol for an audit involving canon status, a proposed creative change,
continuity disposition, release review, or provenance. Load it after any eligible
protected first-use pass and `product-context.md`. It adds a focused evidence
record, not a scorecard or authority to approve anything.

Identify the exact world/property, branch or working context, subject, version or
hash, current authoritative source, visible actor/role, and proposed action.
Inspect current contracts and rendered behavior before assigning meaning to a label.
Mark states outside accepted scope not implemented or not applicable; do not invent them.

## Test state legibility

Check whether a creator can identify state, transition, decision owner, and
time/version without inferring from color or location:

- **Idea:** exploratory material with no submitted decision request.
- **Proposal:** a specific suggested change awaiting an authorized decision.
- **Draft:** editable work that is not accepted canon by itself.
- **Working canon:** current editable or branch truth, not an immutable canon release.
- **Approved canon:** exact human-authorized state, not implied release or publication.
- **Release candidate:** an assembled exact version under review, not released.
- **Released:** exact accepted snapshot or package, not automatic publication.
- **Superseded:** retained history replaced by an identified later version, not current.
- **Unresolved:** an open question, contradiction, unknown result, or pending
  decision that must not look accepted or clean.

Test mixed and stale states across links, lists, details, badges, action labels,
confirmations, and receipts. Record visible label and authoritative state source
separately. Flag labels reused across authority layers or lacking authoritative identity.

## Trace why, source, and authorship

For each proposed change, check whether the creator can reach:

- why it is proposed, its evidence or creative intent, the diff, and initiator;
- source material, applicable prompt/import/derivation context, creation time,
  and exact source or parent version without exposing sensitive data;
- a legible origin category: **human-authored**, **AI-suggested**,
  **imported**, or **derived from an approved prior asset**; and
- later human edits, transformations, merges, and provenance gaps without
  erasing earlier origin.

Rationale explains why; provenance explains origin and transformation. AI
fluency, confidence, or a human edit never makes model output self-authorized
canon. Imported or derived material does not inherit approval.

## Preview affected creative work

Before approval, inspect whether the interface names direct, indirect, and
unknown effects on exact versions of:

- characters and relationships;
- arcs and narrative units;
- assets and approved prior assets;
- productions and their pinned canon releases;
- continuity findings/decisions and prior human dispositions; and
- release candidates, releases, packages, and downstream review decisions.

Include places, rules, chronology, or other world truth when implicated. Show why
each item may be affected and whether it needs revalidation, revision, approval,
repinning, or supersession. Distinguish “checked, none” from not evaluated,
unavailable, stale, or unknown.

## Keep contradictions visible

Check that contradictions are visibly **unresolved**, **resolved**, or hidden from
the current view. Resolved items need evidence and a human disposition; resolution
must not imply an unrecorded canon change. Hidden or filtered items need a reachable
count/status and must reappear in relevant impact and approval previews. Unknown,
unavailable, or stale evaluation must fail closed rather than look contradiction-free.

## Test the pre-approval consequence preview

Immediately before a canon-change or release decision, verify the creator can tell:

1. the exact action, subject, version/hash, diff, authority host, and human
   role;
2. the state transition and what will become working canon, approved canon, a
   release candidate, or released;
3. which affected work, pins, findings, approvals, or releases change, remain
   unchanged, require review, or become superseded;
4. which contradictions or unknown effects remain, including hidden items;
5. whether the action is reversible, how revision/supersession works, and what
   durable receipt will prove the decision; and
6. that release approval does not itself grant external publication authority.

Block consequential action when identity, permission, impact, continuity, or outcome
is stale, unavailable, malformed, or ambiguous. Never approve merely to complete a
read-only audit; inspect the preview and use safe fixtures, existing tests, or
recorded receipts for post-action evidence.

## Record evidence

For each subject, record visible state, authoritative source, rationale,
provenance chain, affected-work coverage, contradiction visibility,
pre-approval consequence, evidence label, and uncertainty. Report gaps with
`critique.md`, prioritizing false authority and unsafe approval consequences.
