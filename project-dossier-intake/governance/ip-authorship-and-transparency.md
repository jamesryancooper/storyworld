# IP, Authorship, and AI Transparency Strategy

Name clearance, copyright and human-authorship provenance, contributor agreements, entity structure, and the brand-level AI-transparency stance. Owner-endorsed direction pending decision records. Target canonical homes: chapter 05 (rights and consent), chapter 02 (workflows — property creation), and chapter 01 (product and umbrella context).

**Scope note**: engineering and product planning input, not legal advice. Trademark clearance, copyright registration strategy, and entity structure need counsel; this document defines what the platform and process must support so counsel's advice has somewhere to land.

## 1. Name and trademark clearance

The catalog currently carries ten working titles awaiting names, and several established names that have never been screened. Naming becomes a governed pipeline step rather than an aesthetic afterthought:

1. **Candidate names** proposed (with the property's commercial classes in mind: entertainment services, printed matter, card/board games, education services, music, apparel/merchandise as applicable).
2. **Knockout search** — federal trademark registers, common-law/web usage, domains, social handles, app stores; results recorded as evidence.
3. **Clearance decision** — accept, revise, or drop; recorded as a decision with the search evidence attached.
4. **Registration decision** — whether/when to file, in which classes; tracked in the rights layer with renewal dates.

Specific items for the first pass:

* The ten working titles (Porch Pirates, Tales from Two Kingdoms, The Night Signal, The Crossroads Serial, Twenty-Four Doors, The Long Table, The Homestead Years, Ascension, The Corner Emporium, Matters of Record) — all are placeholders until cleared.
* **Dumpster Fire Friends** — never screened. The property is a Garbage Pail Kids-style parody; the name is distinct, but card layout and trade dress must keep deliberate distance from the active original property, and parody positioning should be reviewed with counsel before commercial release.
* **Established property names** (Stillhouse Archive, BeKindRewind / Rewind Plaza, The Lanterns, Humans of the Future, Commerce Foundry, Storyworld itself, Stavium, Creative Media Ventures) — screen before external-facing use hardens.
* Naming collisions are also internal: property names must stay distinct from fixture names and from each other across the portfolio.

## 2. Human-authorship provenance for copyrightability

Purely AI-generated material without human creative control is not registrable for copyright under current US guidance, and registration applications must disclose AI-generated content. The portfolio's value depends on protectable works, so authorship evidence becomes a platform output:

* **Authorship dossier per work** — a generated derivative compiling the recorded human creative acts for a release: the human-authored source texts (the fairytale draft is human-authored — the registrable spine of that book), briefs and direction given, selection among candidates, revision instructions, arrangement and sequencing decisions, and acceptance receipts. Storyworld already records all of these events; the new capability is compiling them into a registration-ready evidence package.
* **AI-contribution disclosure fields** per release — which elements are AI-generated, AI-assisted, or human-authored — feeding both registration disclosures and the transparency stance in section 5.
* **Practice guidance baked into workflows**: keep human-authored text, outlines, and direction as first-class source records; prefer documented human selection/arrangement over unreviewed bulk acceptance; the platform's existing rule that nothing becomes canon without human acceptance is also the authorship story.

## 3. Contributor and collaborator agreements

Family collaboration is a feature of the portfolio (co-written anthology stories, family photographs in editorial series, potential voice contributions). Each contributor relationship gets a simple written agreement before contributions are accepted:

* Ownership or license of the contribution (assignment vs. license; scope, territory, duration).
* Attribution expectations.
* Revocation handling — what happens to published work if a contributor withdraws (ties to the existing rights-expiry pattern and the fan-contribution boundary's permission model).
* **Minors as contributors** — parental consent for any minor's contribution or likeness, aligned with `childrens-audience-compliance.md`.
* Storage: agreements live in the rights layer as consent records attached to the contributor and their contributions.

## 4. Entity and umbrella structure (open owner decisions)

Currently "Creative Media Ventures" is a one-line strategic concept and Stavium is the platform context; no decision records cover:

* Which legal entity owns property IP (per-property, one media entity, or the platform entity).
* The relationship between the operating/platform entity and the creative properties (internal licensing or common ownership).
* Where trademark registrations and copyright registrations are held.
* How family contributors relate to the owning entity (section 3 agreements name the counterparty).

These are counsel-supported owner decisions; the platform requirement is only that the rights layer can record an owner entity per property and per registration.

## 5. Brand-level AI-transparency stance

Per-work synthetic-media disclosure already exists in the rights layer; what is missing is the public position. Proposed stance, tuned for family- and faith-centered audiences where authenticity is the currency:

* **Stories are human-authored; production is AI-assisted; we say so plainly.** A short standard disclosure per property class (books, video, audio, cards) rather than per-post legalese.
* **Never present AI-generated depictions of real people or real family moments as photographs or recordings of real events** — extends the existing dignity and likeness controls from a review rule to a public commitment.
* Channel-required AI-content flags are automated through adaptors (several platforms now require synthetic-media labeling; the adaptor owns each channel's mechanism).
* The stance is versioned brand policy: properties may tighten it (e.g., the documentary property discloses evidence handling too) but never loosen it below the portfolio floor.

## 6. Decision items for the owner pass

1. Adopt the naming/clearance pipeline as part of property creation; run the first-pass screen list in section 1.
2. Adopt the authorship-dossier derivative and AI-contribution disclosure fields as platform capabilities.
3. Adopt the contributor-agreement requirement and its rights-layer storage; prepare the family-collaborator template with counsel.
4. Decide the entity/umbrella structure with counsel; record it and the IP-ownership rule.
5. Adopt the AI-transparency stance and the standard disclosure language set.
6. Sequence: clearance for any property before its first external commercial release; authorship dossiers from the first registrable work (the fairytale book is the natural pilot).
