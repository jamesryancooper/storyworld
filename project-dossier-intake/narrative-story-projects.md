# Narrative and story projects

This document is the inventory of narrative and story projects to be created and managed in Storyworld. Projects are organized by type. Each entry records the project's type, status, concept, structure, content, audience, delivery format, visual identity, content pipeline, and how it would be built and managed in Storyworld. Fields marked **To be defined** are open owner decisions, not omissions. Names marked **(working title)** are placeholders awaiting owner naming.

Every platform pattern — the fourteen post-F1 pattern fixtures and the eight schema probes — has a designated showcase project; entries that carry a pattern include a **Pattern showcase** field, and the full mapping appears in the "Pattern showcase coverage" section near the end. Patterns this catalog surfaced that the platform does not yet cover are recorded separately under "Proposed additional patterns" and remain proposals until they pass decision records. Platform features referenced by these projects (art-style definitions, export adaptors) are specified separately in `additional-features.md`.

### Stillhouse Archive

* **Type**: Serialized liminal archival mystery.
* **Status**: Established — deeply designed.
* **Concept**: A serialized archival mystery in which followers act as witnesses or investigators: evidence artifacts surface over time, and the audience pieces together a hidden truth that the archive never states outright.
* **Structure**: Serialized mini-seasons; a reveal-dependency graph governs what may surface when; a symbol dictionary and chronology maintain long-form continuity; hidden truth is tracked separately from audience-visible truth; mystery pacing and ARG-safety rules bound the experience.
* **Content**: Evidence artifacts (documents, images, records), serialized posts, symbol and chronology records.
* **Audience**: Followers who enjoy participatory mystery — engaging as witnesses and investigators.
* **Delivery format**: Instagram-first serialized releases; an authorized adaptation program extends the property into an audio-drama adaptation of a mini-season and a youth edition.
* **Visual identity**: To be defined.
* **Content pipeline**: To be defined.
* **Pattern showcase**: Nonlinear chronology — evidence surfaces out of story order by design, so story time, presentation order, and publication time stay strictly separate, with spoiler calculations for out-of-order releases. Also carries alternate canon and adaptation branches through the adaptation program: adaptations may composite minor characters or adjust presentation for their audience, and none of those changes may alter source canon.
* **In Storyworld**: Property workspace with reveal graphs, symbol dictionary, and chronology as first-class records; creator truth versus audience knowledge tracked per release; continuity views for what-is-known-when; adaptation branches carry explicit ancestry and stay version-pinned to the source canon they adapt.

## Participatory and event-driven narratives

### The Crossroads Serial (working title)

* **Type**: Audience-directed serialized fiction.
* **Status**: Concept — generated as the showcase project for the audience-directed serialization pattern.
* **Concept**: A wayfarer's journey reaches a genuine crossroads at the end of each episode, and the audience votes on which of two fully authored continuations happens. The road taken becomes canon; the road not taken is preserved as alternate material. Choices and their consequences are the story's engine — fiction in the portfolio's wisdom register.
* **Structure**: Episodes ending on decision points; two pre-approved continuation episodes authored per decision; poll → moderation → close → human authorization → canon acceptance; non-winning material archived, never leaked as spoilers.
* **Content**: Serialized episodes; decision framing posts; archived alternate episodes.
* **Audience**: Social audiences who want agency in a story; readers drawn to choice-and-consequence narratives.
* **Delivery format**: Social serial with native poll mechanics, via export adaptors.
* **Visual identity**: To be defined.
* **Content pipeline**: Author both continuations → approve both → run poll window → moderate and close → human accepts winner into canon → archive alternate.
* **Pattern showcase**: Audience-directed serialization — audience input distinct from audience authority; poll windows, moderation, manipulation handling, spoiler management, and mandatory human authorization.
* **In Storyworld**: Pre-approved branch pairs per decision point; poll results recorded as external signals that never change canon without a human decision; alternate-material archive.

* **Pattern showcase**: Evidence-based nonfiction and post-publication correction — claims linked to evidence, conflicting sources, confidence and uncertainty, quote and excerpt rights, correction versus clarification versus retraction, published-version preservation, and downstream derivative invalidation.

## Proposed additional patterns (pending decision records)

Five narrative patterns surfaced by this catalog are not yet covered by the platform's pattern set. They are recorded here as proposals with designated showcase properties — none requires a new property. Per the dossier process, each needs a decision record before becoming contract work; they are deliberately kept out of the per-project **Pattern showcase** fields and coverage tables above, which list accepted patterns only.

* **Nested narrative and diegetic levels** — content that is canon *as a text* while its events are not canon *as events*: tales characters tell, parables, in-world books. Must track which diegetic level any fact belongs to, keep inner-story facts from leaking into world canon, and let inner stories carry their own motifs and continuity. Planned rejection: an event from a told tale is treated as having happened in the world. **Showcase**: The Long Table (a frame-tale anthology by construction) and Notes to My Daughter (parables and evolving metaphors); The Night Signal's on-air stories as secondary coverage.
* **In-fiction parallel timelines and loops** — cosmologies that legitimately contain coexisting timelines, repeated days, or a multiverse, kept strictly distinct from production branches (adaptations, proposals, experiments). Must address entity state at a (timeline, iteration, story-time) coordinate, cross-timeline identity, and audience knowledge per timeline. Planned rejection: a story timeline is modeled as a workflow branch, and a production merge collapses two canonical timelines. **Showcase**: The Lanterns; the Metaphysical project when it takes form.
* **Retcon lifecycle** — deliberate revision of published canon as a first-class operation, distinct from forking an alternate canon, adapting, and production-local simplification. Must cover superseding an accepted fact, impact analysis across already-published units, errata versus in-world justification, and preservation of the superseded record. Also adds the missing fiction counterpart to the metamorphic transformation list ("retcon a canon fact"). Planned rejection: a retconned fact silently rewrites published units or erases the record that the old fact was ever canon. **Showcase**: Stillhouse Archive; The Lanterns as secondary.
* **Transmedia complementary canon** — different, complementary canon fragments distributed across media and channels, where the full picture exists only across them; audience knowledge must be computable per channel subset ("what does an album-only listener know?"). Distinct from adaptation, which retells the *same* story per medium. Planned rejection: a spoiler calculation assumes every audience member follows every channel. **Showcase**: Ascension paired with the visual advice-storytelling property; The Lanterns as a candidate.
* **Community and fan contribution boundary** — audience-*submitted* material (distinct from audience input such as votes) with contributor permission, attribution, moderation, revocation, and explicit non-canon status. Elevates the extension deferred under the collaborative-merge pattern into a scheduled pattern of its own. Planned rejection: a fan submission, or its ideas, enters canon without permission, attribution, and an authorized human acceptance. **Showcase**: Stillhouse Archive (witness submissions in the ARG register); The Crossroads Serial as secondary.
